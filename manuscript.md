Does Digital Transformation Undermine Gastronomic Authenticity? The Mediating Role of Perceived Gastronomic Authenticity in Tourist Loyalty within a 
World Heritage City





Abstract

The study investigates the mediating role of perceived gastronomic authenticity in the relationship between digital transformation—encompassing both urban and restaurant dimensions—and visitor satisfaction, social value, and destination recommendations. Utilizing a sample of 331 visitors to a World Heritage City, the research employed partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) to analyze a reflective model. The results reveal that gastronomic authenticity initiates a causal sequence that significantly influences social value, satisfaction, and tourist loyalty, establishing it as a central component of the experience in heritage destinations. This work represents the first empirical examination that simultaneously addresses both dimensions of digitalization as determinants of perceived gastronomic authenticity within the context of Andalusian culinary heritage, offering valuable insights for destination managers and enhancing the positioning of gastronomic identity in the global tourism market.

Keywords: gastronomic authenticity; digital transformation; heritage tourism; tourist loyalty; PLS-SEM; Andalusian culinary heritage; Córdoba; UNESCO

1. Introduction 
Al-Andalus, a term used between the eighth and fifteenth centuries to describe part of present-day southern Spain (García Fernández, 2022), stands as one of the most influential historical and cultural legacies of the Iberian Peninsula. This legacy is prominently reflected today in Andalusia, where the region's gastronomy—rooted in Andalusian traditions—represents a complex cultural system that transcends mere culinary practice and becomes a fundamental pillar of intangible cultural heritage (Andriyansah & Sukendri, 2025; Martins-Rodal & Patiño-Romarís, 2025). The origins of this culinary richness are linked to the "Umayyad green revolution," characterized by advanced irrigation techniques and the introduction of new crops, such as rice, citrus fruits, eggplant, and sugar cane, which facilitated the exchange of knowledge between Eastern and Western Mediterranean cultures (Vaquerizo Gil, 2017; Jiménez-Brobeil et al., 2022). This process also resulted from the syncretism between Muslims, Christians, and Jews (Salas-Salvadó et al., 2006), which Martínez de Victoria Muñoz et al. (2019) identify as the greatest historical influence on traditional Spanish recipes.
The solidity of this heritage relies not solely on literary sources but is also corroborated by significant material evidence. Archaeobotanical findings from Islamic suburbs reveal the enduring consumption of cereals, legumes, and fruits, which continue to underpin the current Mediterranean diet (Coradeschi et al., 2017; Zimmerer et al., 2024). This historical continuity positions Córdoba—as the former capital of the Umayyad caliphate and a designated World Heritage Site (García García, 2023)—as an exceptional context for analyzing gastronomic authenticity as an experiential construct. Literature has established cultural heritage as a "strategic cornerstone" for socio-economic development (Sgroi, 2023) and as a source of territorial competitive advantage (Seyitoğlu and Ivanov, 2020). Within this framework, the modern tourist approaches the destination as a hedonic traveler, seeking memorable experiences that transcend the mere act of eating (Flavián et al., 2018; Li et al., 2023).
Engaging with the local culinary ecosystem fosters the development of emotional connections with the territory (Huang et al., 2013; Rachão et al., 2021) and enables visitors to reconnect with the genius loci through flavors. In this context, gastronomy serves as a mechanism to "place the locale on the plate," integrating recipes, techniques, and local products to enhance territorial identity amid global homogenization (Čaušević & Fusté-Forné, 2022; Massari et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024). This immersive experience, which blends heritage, landscape, and identity, positions gastronomic authenticity as the central axis of the tourist experience, connecting local elements with the destination's global brand (Cavic et al., 2024; Del Soldato & Massari, 2024; Martins-Rodal & Patiño-Romarís, 2025). Understanding this is particularly crucial in a context where digitalization is reshaping both urban spaces and the restaurant sector.
On the other hand, digital technology has emerged as a pivotal mediator in the contemporary tourist experience. Within the posttourism paradigm, it enables visitors to engage with destinations in advance through processes of "simulated mobility" (Carrasco-García et al., 2022). In the context of Tourism 4.0 (Starc-Peceny et al., 2019), gastronomic tourists begin to interact with their chosen destinations long before their actual arrival, shaping their expectations through social networks and digital platforms (Zamarreño Aramendia et al., 2021; Bu et al., 2021; Martins-Rodal & Patiño-Romarís, 2025). When there is alignment between a destination's genuine gastronomic identity and its digital representation, tourist satisfaction tends to increase, thereby reducing the potential for dissonance between expectations and actual experiences (Oliver, 1980; Zamarreño Aramendia et al., 2021; Bo, 2026). From this viewpoint, digitalization is no longer viewed as a threat to authenticity; rather, it is recognized as a strategic enabler when implemented with cultural sensitivity (Del Soldato & Massari, 2024; Andriyansah & Sukendri, 2025).
In this context, Smart Cities and Smart Tourist Destinations utilize tools such as e-storytelling, QR codes, and augmented reality to create connections between archaeological evidence and contemporary tourism products (Del Soldato & Massari, 2024; Khanchai et al., 2025). These technologies enhance perceived authenticity by fostering an emotional connection between visitors and the identity of the territory (Schimperna et al., 2021; Andriyansah & Sukendri, 2025). Thus, digitalization operates on two interdependent levels (Buhalis & Amaranggana, 2015; Schimperna et al., 2021). On an urban scale, mobile applications and augmented reality tools support the cultural interpretation of destinations; at the restaurant level, QR codes and storytelling strategies enhance the heritage value of dishes through a blend of traditional and digital elements (Del Soldato & Massari, 2024; Lankauskas et al., 2025).
This digital ecosystem influences both the on-site experience  and the formation of prior expectations (Carrasco-García et al., 2022; Bo, 2026). Content generated by other users has superior credibility to institutional marketing, as it is perceived as independent of commercial interests (Amalia & Hanika, 2021; Chawla et al., 2024). When authenticity is perceived as genuine, it activates a social value of a symbolic nature linked to self-concept, social recognition, and cultural belonging (Sharafuddin et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2024), manifesting itself in the willingness to share, recommend, and generate  positive e-WOM (Shahrin & Hussin, 2023; Cavic et al., 2024; Ramos & Pinto, 2024). Previous studies confirm that local gastronomy has a positive impact on tourist satisfaction and loyalty, reinforcing its suitability as a case study (Hernández-Rojas et al., 2021; Calderón-Fajardo et al., 2024; Uslu et al., 2024). 
The digitalization of gastronomic heritage also creates tensions. There is a risk of cultural homogenization and the dilution of identity markers that are considered authentic (Mkono, 2012; Zamarreño Aramendia et al., 2021; Shahrin & Hussin, 2023). This paradox, which involves balancing innovation with the preservation of cultural essence—lies at the heart of the study and necessitates management models centered on eco-digital resilience. This concept refers to the ability to integrate technology and sustainability without compromising cultural values (Vrontis et al., 2021; Andronic & Untaru, 2025; Andriyansah & Sukendri, 2025).
However, culinary digitalization, when oriented towards criteria of efficiency, global visibility, or adaptation to dominant trends of the tourism market, can lead to a progressive loss of the local gastronomic essence. The standardization of recipes, the simplification of traditional processes and the reinterpretation of the culinary offer to meet externalized expectations can dilute the identity, symbolic and cultural values that sustain gastronomic authenticity. In this context, gastronomy runs the risk of becoming a homogeneous and decontextualized experiential product, which not only negatively affects the authenticity perceived by the tourist, but can also harm the differentiation of the destination and, ultimately, its ability to generate sustainable tourism loyalty. From a theoretical perspective, this work advances in the conceptualization of gastronomic authenticity as an experiential construct influenced by technological mediation (Shahrin & Hussin, 2023; Kovalenko et al., 2023), integrating urban digitalization and restaurant digitalization into a single model, as well as their effects on social value, satisfaction and loyalty. Methodologically, the study adopts a quantitative approach based on surveys of gastronomic tourists in Cordoba, following consolidated lines in the literature (Hernández-Rojas et al., 2021; Massari et al., 2023). This design allows for the capture of complex symbolic constructs that are difficult to apprehend in culturally dense contexts (Wang, 1999; Huete-Alcocer & Hernández-Rojas, 2022; Kovalenko et al., 2023).
Finally, the study addresses a relevant gap in the literature by analyzing how the digital maturity of the gastronomic offer influences the perceived authenticity, satisfaction, social value and loyalty of the tourist in a Heritage city. The research questions posed allow us to generate relevant implications for tourism management, offering an operational framework that reconciles the preservation of the Andalusian legacy with an authentic and digitally mediated gastronomic experience (Schuhbauer & Hausmann, 2022; Andronic & Untaru, 2025).
2. Conceptual framework
Recent literature suggests that urban digitalization is today a fundamental pillar in shaping the tourist experience, transforming cities into Smart Tourist Destinations (DTIs) through technological infrastructures and artificial intelligence (Botezat et al., 2024). In this context, the digitalization of the city and the restaurant should not be understood as a mere operational tool, but as a "curator" of history (Andriyansah & Sukendri, 2025). This digitized environment not only improves accessibility but also facilitates a deeper cultural interpretation of local heritage (Centrih et al., 2025). Thus, technology acts as an enabler that allows tourists to explore the destination, reducing uncertainty and strengthening their emotional connection with the place (Nawawi et al., 2024).
In parallel, the digitalization of the restaurant has emerged as a determining factor of the culinary experience, by integrating tools ranging from interactive menus to advanced management systems (Hu & Min, 2024). In contexts of high heritage value, such as that of Andalusian gastronomy, these tools allow a narrative of authenticity to be projected based on scientific and archaeobotanical evidence, which makes it easier for tourists to perceive a real link with the medieval legacy of Al-Andalus (Vaquerizo Gil, 2017). Such technological implementation offers a rich narrative around traceability and traditional methods, reinforcing diner confidence (Segovia-Villarreal & Rosa-Díaz, 2022).
Finally, perceived gastronomic authenticity is consolidated as a central construct that influences tourist satisfaction and loyalty (Berdejo-Fariña & Espino-Rodríguez, 2026). When the culinary offer reflects the local identity, the intention to generate positive e-WOM increases (Julian et al., 2024). Under this framework, the following hypotheses are proposed:
2.1. The digitalisation of the restaurant and the city and its relationship with perceived gastronomic authenticity.
The relationship between the digitalization of the restaurant and perceived gastronomic authenticity is based on the ability of technological tools to act as mediators that enrich the cultural value of the culinary experience (Berdejo-Fariña & Espino-Rodríguez, 2024). Innovation should not be conceived only as a functional advance, but as a strategic mechanism that reinforces the perception of authenticity by facilitating the traceability and enhancement of local products (García García, 2023). 
Recent research shows that the functional quality of the digital service positively influences perceived authenticity, as long as the tools operate reliably (Song et al., 2025). Technology allows a richer narrative to be projected about traditional methods and the origin of ingredients, which increases the diner's trust and sense of connection with the establishment (Kumar et al., 2026). Likewise, the use of immersive technologies, such as augmented reality, enables the creation of sensory environments that enhance the symbolic meaning of gastronomy, transforming service into an authentic experience (Kshetri, 2024). However, for the impact to be positive, there must be a perceived fit between the technology and the type of cuisine; in local cuisine restaurants, technology must complement the human factor to prevent dehumanization from eroding genuine hospitality (Albreht Centih et al., 2024). Under these premises, the following hypothesis is proposed: 
H1: The digitalization of the restaurant has a positive influence on the perceived gastronomic authenticity.
The scientific literature argues that the configuration of Smart Tourist Destinations (DTIs), through the use of technological infrastructures such as augmented reality (AR), mobile applications and geolocation systems, is essential to enrich the cultural interpretation of local heritage (Elshaer et al. (2022). As Giménez Manuel et al. (2025) point out, in cities with a deep historical legacy, such as Andalusia, urban digitalization not only improves accessibility to information, but also acts as a mediator that facilitates a more intense emotional connection with the territory and its history, reducing visitor uncertainty (Miletić et al.,  2025).
The phenomenon of digital culture on social networks – such as Instagram or YouTube – shows that tourists, by sharing visual and geolocated content, act as co-creators of the image of the destination, reinforcing the perception that the experience is genuine (Pinhal et al., 2025). Specifically, the use of digital platforms makes it possible to project a narrative of gastronomic authenticity based on traceability and traditional methods, which is essential for an identity resource as complex as Andalusian cuisine, characterised by its hybridism and cultural symbolism (García García, 2023). Recent studies confirm that exposure to digital content and electronic recommendations (e-WOM) positively influences social value and visitor confidence in the local culinary offer (Kumar et al., 2026). In this way, urban technology is presented as a catalyst that elevates perceived authenticity by coherently integrating technological modernity with the preservation of the destination's historical-gastronomic heritage (Berdejo-Fariña & Espino-Rodríguez, 2024). The result is the following hypothesis:
H2: The digitalisation of the city has a positive influence on perceived gastronomic authenticity.
2.2. Gastronomic authenticity and its relationship with social value.
Perceived gastronomic authenticity acts as a catalyst that significantly elevates the social value of the tourism experience, transforming food consumption into an act of cultural mediation and human connection (Vujić et al., 2024). Gastronomy is not only a basic need, but also a manifestation of the social and cultural heritage that allows tourists to get closer to the identity and customs of the destination visited (Menor-Campos et al., 2022).
Social value is manifested, first of all, through the sense of connection and belonging that authenticity generates. By participating in traditional culinary experiences, the visitor experiences a deep social and cultural interaction that allows them to genuinely approach the daily life, history, and values of the local community (Huete-Alcocer & Hernández-Rojas, 2022). This immersion favors the creation of emotional bonds between tourists and residents, based on respect for tradition and culture (Uslu et al., 2023).
Secondly, authenticity provides symbolic value and prestige. The contemporary tourist seeks experiences that express their identity and lifestyle, using authenticity as a tool of social distinction. Sharing these experiences—either through direct interaction with friends and family or through the dissemination of "authentic" images on social networks—reinforces the individual's social positioning and projects a sophisticated and differentiated lifestyle (Michael & Fusté-Forné, 2022).
According to scientific literature, when gastronomy is perceived as genuine, it ceases to be a simple product to become a vehicle for social integration. This allows tourists to satisfy higher-order needs related to self-realization and recognition, which consolidates the social value associated with their experience in the destination (Huete-Alcocer & Hernández-Rojas, 2022). In this way, the following hypothesis is constructed:
H3: Perceived gastronomic authenticity positively influences the social value associated with the tourist experience.
2.3. Gastronomic authenticity perceived as a determinant of tourist satisfaction and loyalty.
The consideration of authenticity as a central element of the tourist experience, as it acts as a stimulus that generates favorable emotional and cognitive responses (Bakr et al., 2025). Gastronomic authenticity is conceived as a multidimensional construct that integrates tangible components —use of local ingredients and preservation of traditional culinary techniques— and intangible components —atmosphere of the establishment and quality of service— (Hernández-Rojas & Huete-Alcocer, 2021). From the theory of cognitive appraisal, satisfaction judgments are elaborated from these emotional reactions; when the gastronomic offer faithfully reflects the culture of the destination, the level of satisfaction tends to increase (Zhu et al., 2024).
Three mechanisms are identified that explain this positive relationship. First, the cultural connection that emerges from an authentic culinary experience allows tourists to approach the destination in greater depth, satisfying their motivation for learning and increasing the perception of value (Vukolić et al., 2023). Secondly, the sensory and emotional quality associated with the use of local products reinforces the perception of freshness and increases sensory pleasure, directly impacting the well-being experienced during the visit (Yang et al., 2024). Third, authenticity contributes to reducing uncertainty, generating confidence in the destination's commitment to quality and strengthening the global image that tourists build from their experience (Durmaz et al., 2022). Together, these mechanisms support that perceived authenticity transforms the act of eating into a meaningful cultural experience, capable of boosting both global satisfaction and loyalty to the destination, consequently, the hypothesis is formulated:
H4: Perceived gastronomic authenticity positively influences tourist satisfaction.
With respect to perceived authenticity, it positively and significantly influences tourist loyalty, acting as a critical determinant of the competitiveness and sustainability of destinations (Rini et al., 2024). This construct is manifested primarily through object-related authenticity (truthfulness of the tangible) and existential authenticity (the personal and genuine connection to experience), both of which are fundamental driving dimensions of future behavioral intention (Luo et al., 2024).
Studies developed in heritage tourism destinations demonstrate that the objective authenticity of the main attractions and the existential authenticity derived from participation in living rituals or traditions are the strongest predictors of visitor retention (Zhang et al., 2021). While the former validates the originality of the environment, the latter facilitates processes of self-discovery that consolidate the tourist's commitment to the destination (Zhang & Hedayati Marzbali, 2024), resulting in a conative and attitudinal loyalty expressed in the desire to return and recommend the place (Uslu et al., 2024).
Key mechanisms such as attachment to place and tourist engagement mediate this relationship, allowing the perception of the "authentic" to translate into lasting loyalty (Wang et al., 2022; Luo et al., 2024). Likewise, cultural identity acts as a lens that amplifies the value of authenticity, intensifying loyalty in visitors who feel a deep affinity with the heritage on display (Rather, 2018). In conclusion, perceived authenticity reduces uncertainty, strengthens trust towards the destination (Jebbouri et al., 2022), and fosters a psychological connection that transforms a casual visit into a long-term relationship.
H5: Perceived authenticity positively influences tourist loyalty.
2.4. Tourist satisfaction as a determinant of loyalty to the gastronomic destination.
Tourism satisfaction is defined as an emotional response derived from a cognitive process in which the visitor contrasts the perceived performance of the experience with their previous expectations. In the context of consumer behavior, this variable constitutes a critical antecedent of loyalty, manifested through the intention to return and the willingness to generate positive recommendations (WOM/e-WOM). In the field of gastronomic tourism, satisfaction with local cuisine functions as a mechanism of emotional attachment that transforms the image of the destination (Durmaz et al., 2022; Kovalenko et al., 2023). The literature identifies key dimensions in this process: the sensory quality and freshness of the food, the hospitality of the staff, and the atmosphere of the establishment (Vujić et al., 2024; Martínez-Roget, 2024), elements that reinforce the perception of authenticity. Especially in destinations with heritage value, the discovery of culture through intangible heritage acts as a driver of loyalty. This relationship has been empirically validated in various contexts such as Spain (Hernández-Rojas & Huete-Alcocer, 2021), Peru (Esparza Huamanchumo et al., 2023), Pakistan (Ullah et al., 2022) and Turkey (Kodaş & Özel, 2023), consolidating the satisfactory experience as the strongest predictor of the tourist's long-term engagement:
H6: Tourist satisfaction positively influences their loyalty to the destination or dining experience.

3 Methodology

3.1 Research context	

Gastronomic tourism in destinations with a strong heritage component has experienced sustained growth in recent decades, consolidating itself as a modality capable of articulating the preservation of cultural identity with the generation of economic and social value for local communities (Martins-Rodal & Patiño Romarís, 2025). In this context, destinations with a historically documented and culturally rooted culinary heritage enjoy a unique competitive position, as their gastronomy is difficult to replicate in other territories (Del Soldato & Massari, 2024). Specifically, the city of Cordoba is one of the most representative cases in Spain and the Mediterranean of this convergence between living intangible heritage and identity gastronomy. Its cuisine is not an artificially constructed tourist product, but the daily expression of a millenary cultural heritage, with roots in the Andalusian civilization and uninterrupted continuity to the present day (García García, 2023). This legacy not only has a symbolic value, but also has a material validation supported by archaeobotanical evidence that documents the continued consumption of products present in today's traditional Cordoba gastronomy (Coradeschi et al., 2017). This historical continuity endows local cuisine with a degree of authenticity that is structurally difficult to reproduce in other destinations.
Unlike other heritage cities where the culinary tradition has been reinterpreted for tourism purposes, Córdoba preserves living gastronomic practices – taverns, recipes and local products – directly traceable to al-Andalus (Martínez de Victoria Muñoz et al., 2019; García García, 2023). This uniqueness makes the city an ideal laboratory for analysing perceived gastronomic authenticity and the interaction between heritage preservation and digital mediation. In addition, its progressive incorporation into Smart Tourist Destination models and the growing use of digital tools in restaurants allow us to examine the impact of digitalization on the gastronomic experience, focusing the analysis on tourists whose main motivation is the consumption of traditional local cuisine.

3.2 Methods of analysis and participants

To contrast the relationships proposed in the conceptual model, the Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) technique was used (Hair et al., 2026). This methodology makes it possible to simultaneously evaluate the measurement model – verifying the reliability and convergent and discriminant validity of the constructs – and the structural model, by contrasting the hypotheses formulated. As a prediction-oriented approach, PLS-SEM estimates relationships between latent variables without requiring strict distributional assumptions, which makes it a methodologically sound option for ordinal data from Likert scales, as is the case study.
Unlike covariance-based methods, PLS-SEM maximizes explained variance (R²) and does not require distributional normality, characteristics that make it especially appropriate for the present study (N = 331). The analysis was carried out with SmartPLS 4 (Ringle et al., 2024), estimating external loads and trajectory coefficients. Statistical significance was evaluated by bootstrapping procedure with 5,000 resamplings, which guarantees the robustness of the results and the validity of the proposed model.


3.3. Measurement instrument and questionnaire design

The questionnaire was structured in two parts: the first collected socioeconomic information from the respondents and; the second part consists of 24 items (Table 1) structured on a 5-point Likert scale, where respondents expressed the degree of agreement or disagreement (1=not at all agree to 5=strongly agree). A total of 331 surveys were finally validated. Data collection was carried out between October and November 2025 through two complementary channels: in-house interviewers who administered the questionnaire on digital tablets inside restaurants with gastronomy of Andalusian heritage located in the historic centre of Cordoba, and a leaflet with a QR code deposited in the establishments themselves to facilitate the visitor's autonomous response. Before completing the questionnaire, the participants (> 18 any) were informed that their participation was entirely voluntary and that they could decline to take part or withdraw their consent at any time without needing to provide any justification. The study was conducted in compliance with current legal requirements and in accordance with the ethical principles established in the Declaration of Helsinki. Access to the data collected was restricted exclusively to the research team, whose members are committed to ensuring confidentiality. All data were processed in an aggregated form and analyzed anonymously.

4. Research results

4.1. Characteristics of the tourist

	The sociodemographic profile of the 331 participants reveals a predominance of women, with 204 women (61.6%) compared to 125 men (37.8%), and 2 participants (0.6%) who did not provide this data. In terms of age distribution, the most represented group was 26 to 40 years old (29.3%), followed by 18 to 25 years old (27.5%) and 41 to 55 years old (22.4%), with those over 55 years old being the least numerous group (14.8%). The level of education is high: 46.2% have a bachelor's degree, 28.1% have secondary or baccalaureate education, 14.8% have vocational training and 10.6% have postgraduate studies. As for gastronomic spending, the most frequent bracket was from 20 to 40 euros per person (39.9%), followed by 41 to 60 euros (27.8%) and more than 60 euros (24.2%).

As for the reflective measurement model (Table 2), its evaluation was carried out following the criteria proposed by Hair et al. The six constructs of the model achieve Cronbach's alpha values greater than 0.82, which accredits a satisfactory internal consistency. Composite reliability (rho_c) exceeds the minimum threshold of 0.70 in all constructs, ranging from 0.833 (DIG-C) to 0.971 (DIG-R). On the other hand, the AVE values range from 0.512 (DIG-C) to 0.874 (LOY-R), exceeding the minimum threshold of 0.50 in all cases, which confirms that each construct explains more than half of the variance of its indicators. Overall, these results show that the measurement instrument has adequate psychometric properties, which gives solidity to the structural estimates examined below.

The predictive capacity of the structural model was evaluated using the coefficient of determination (R²) of each endogenous latent variable (Table 5). 5. Discussion

5.1. Implications for research in heritage gastronomic tourism

The results show, firstly, that the digitalisation of the restaurant has a significant, but negative, effect on perceived authenticity (H1: β = −0.220; p = 0.003). This finding is a warning sign for strategists in the sector: technology must be integrated as a narrative complement to culinary identity – through audiovisual storytelling on the Andalusian legacy, QR codes linked to the historical traceability of dishes or augmented reality at points of heritage interest – preserving in any case the human factor that defines genuine hospitality. The absence of a significant effect of urban digitalization on perceived gastronomic authenticity (H2: β = 0.103; p = 0.710) does not imply that this dimension lacks strategic relevance, but that its contribution is not statistically supported in the present study, which suggests that the impact of the destination's digital tools depends on the degree of cultural coherence with which they are designed and implemented (Del Soldato and Massari,  2024).
Secondly, perceived gastronomic authenticity exerts a positive and significant influence on both social value (H3: β = 0.648; p < 0.001) and tourist satisfaction (H4: β = 0.764; p < 0.001). From a practical perspective, these findings constitute a solid argument for tourism managers and owners of catering establishments: the preservation and communication of the Andalusian culinary legacy represent the most effective strategic lever to consolidate both the satisfaction and the social value perceived by the visitor. The new generations demand digitalization, but in heritage destinations this must be at the service of history and identity, not replace them (Almokdad et al., 2024; Albreht Centrih et al., 2025).
Third, perceived authenticity directly influences loyalty to the destination (H5: β = 0.375; p < 0.001), and satisfaction acts as a second determinant of social value, amplifying the effects of the model (H6: SAT → LOY-S: β = 0.523; p < 0.001). Both results underline the need to constantly maintain and improve the quality of the gastronomic experience, both in its sensory and cultural dimensions. A culinary offer that is well founded on the Andalusian legacy can become a motivating factor for visits and revisits, as evidenced by previous studies in the destination itself (Huete-Alcocer & Hernández-Rojas, 2022).
The effects of authenticity are particularly significant. Perceived gastronomic authenticity positively influences both the social value of the experience and visitor satisfaction, the latter being the largest effect of the model. In addition, authenticity has a direct effect on loyalty to the destination, evidencing its central role in tourist loyalty. Finally, satisfaction acts as an additional determinant of social value, reinforcing the symbolic and community component of the gastronomic experience.

5.2. Implications for the management of heritage tourist destinations

The study analyses the relationship between urban and gastronomic digitalisation, perceived authenticity, satisfaction and visitor loyalty in restaurants of Andalusian heritage located in the historic centre of Cordoba, a city declared a World Heritage Site and a benchmark for Mediterranean gastronomy with Islamic and Sephardic roots. This context makes the city an ideal setting to examine how technological integration interacts with the preservation of culinary identity. Six hypotheses were formulated, of which five obtain empirical support.
The results reveal, firstly, that the digitalisation of the restaurant has a significant, albeit negative, effect on perceived authenticity. This finding warns of the risk of poorly integrated digitalisation and underlines the need for technology to function as a narrative complement to the culinary legacy – for example, through storytelling, QR codes or interpretive resources – without replacing the human dimension of hospitality. Urban digitalization, on the other hand, does not show a significant effect on perceived gastronomic authenticity, suggesting that its impact depends on the degree of cultural coherence with which the destination's digital tools are designed and implemented.
Secondly, perceived gastronomic authenticity has a positive and significant influence on both the social value of the experience and on the satisfaction of the tourist, consolidating itself as the main driver of the gastronomic experience. These results reinforce the strategic importance of preserving and communicating the Andalusian culinary legacy, especially in heritage destinations where digitalisation must be at the service of identity and not replace it.
Finally, perceived authenticity directly influences loyalty to the destination, while satisfaction acts as an additional determinant that amplifies this effect. Overall, the model shows that a gastronomy of cultural heritage, articulated through culturally sensitive digitalisation strategies, contributes to increasing satisfaction, reinforcing the social value of the experience and fostering visitor loyalty, underlining the need for comprehensive and interdisciplinary management of the destination.

6. Conclusions

The results show that, in cities with strong cultural heritage, the integration of culturally sensitive digital tools in the gastronomic offer increases visitor satisfaction, reinforces perceived authenticity and projects a more solid and differentiated destination image. The study fills a gap in the literature by jointly analysing urban digitalisation and that of catering establishments as antecedents of the gastronomic authenticity perceived in destinations with Andalusian heritage. Using a PLS-SEM model based on a sample of 331 gastronomic tourists in Cordoba, it is confirmed that perceived authenticity surpasses digitalization as a predictor of satisfaction and loyalty. In highly symbolic contexts, technology only adds value when it reinforces cultural identity and does not replace genuine hospitality.
Likewise, gastronomic authenticity activates a causal sequence that has an impact on social value, satisfaction and tourist loyalty, consolidating itself as the central axis of the experience in heritage destinations. In Cordoba, visitors especially value the coherence between the cultural story and the culinary proposal, the use of local products and the preservation of traditional elaborations, which generates symbolic social value and positive e-WOM. From an applied perspective, the results offer managers and restaurateurs a framework to integrate digitalisation as a narrative resource at the service of culinary identity, although its scope is limited to the specific context of the destination analysed.
